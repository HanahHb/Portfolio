I’m a Lebanese American third culture kid who grew up in Abu Dhabi, and a development engineer working to understand water security and the power dynamics that shape how communities access and manage natural resources.

My work supports sustainable, community-led projects that give people autonomy over their natural environments, because communities are healthiest when they have a say over the resources they depend on. My mission is to reduce the burden that water insecurity places on women’s daily lives.

My story

When the tap stops running

Most of us turn on a tap without wondering where the water comes from, until it stops. Growing up between Abu Dhabi and Lebanon, I learned early that water reaches our homes through electricity. When power returned after hours of cuts, someone on the hillside would shout, “Ijit il kahraba!” (the electricity is back). It meant the fridge worked again, and that we could finally shower.

At a borehole in Zambia, I understood what my grandmother meant when she described drawing water from a well during Lebanon’s civil war, to bathe five children and make tea to keep them warm at night. Watching mothers do the same work every day, I finally grasped the responsibility she had carried.

Water running from a tap is a privilege. My work, including my capstone, is driven by a simple aim: make mothers’ lives easier by making sure the water keeps running, and so support the generations that follow.

How I work

What development engineering means to me

I understand development as building systems that grow a community’s own autonomy, a view shaped by Amartya Sen’s Development as Freedom.

Empowerment, not enablement

Break dependency structures and look at the whole system, so that support does not deepen existing disparities.

Users first

Start with human-centered design: ask people what works for them and what challenges they face before proposing anything.

Look beyond the technical

Funding requirements, power dynamics and who is affected matter as much as the engineering.

Know my position

I have lived with water outages in Lebanon and Zambia, yet I am an outsider to every community I serve. I test my assumptions with stakeholders throughout.
